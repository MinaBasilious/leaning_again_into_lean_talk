# What is solid and what to check

**Read closely before the talk:** Buzzard's post (four questions, the Fermat
definition trap, the CFSG and OpenAI statistics, the 640 thousand line count),
de Moura's post on the verified kernels, Wiedijk's paper, Axiom Math's
announcement of the CFSG formalization, the Lean Kernel Arena page for the
Navier-Stokes export, and Friedman's essay.

**Partly read:** 
- the Navier-Stokes critique by Bastounis, Circelli and Hansen
(arXiv:2610.08144). It is a preprint, and its authors say they make no claim
about whether OpenAI's written proof is correct.
- Pollack's paper, cited for its framing.

**Seen in search results or summaries, so check them:**
- the Fermat figures (13 million lines, about 11 days) on Anthropic's page
- the details of the Collatz incident beyond what de Moura and Buzzard say, in
  the postmortem itself
- exactly what `lake check`, `lake comparator` and `--paranoid` do, in the
  Lean 4.35 reference
- how con-ron relates to con-leche
- the precise consistency strength Carneiro proves, in his thesis

**Claims by authors, not verified:** Friedman's figures on OpenAI's release (722
articles, about three hours each). The CFSG counterexample and the 34 deviations
are Axiom Math's own report.

**The demo file** is the speaker's own, except the Fermat definition, which is
credited to Buzzard.
